Sink Not Draining but No Clog Found? Avon, IN Causes and Tests

By the Avon 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

The plunger pulled nothing, the snake came back clean, and the basin still sits full. That usually means the blockage is not in the trap you can reach. The most common culprits are a stopper mechanism that no longer lifts, a plumbing vent that cannot let air in behind the water, a dishwasher or disposal connection that is partly closed, or a clog farther down the branch line.

Each one leaves a different fingerprint, and a few simple tests will tell you which you have before you take anything apart.

Why a drain can be blocked with nothing in the pipe

A drain needs two things to move water: a clear path and air. Water leaving a pipe creates a vacuum behind it, and the plumbing vent, the pipe that rises through the roof, lets air in to fill that space. If the vent is blocked, water leaves slowly and noisily, even though the pipe itself is open.

Restrictions can also be partial. A trap with a thin ring of soap and hair lets a trickle through, so a snake passes without catching anything. Plunging a half-open pipe only moves the same buildup back and forth.

Match the symptom to the likely cause

Run water and watch before you disassemble anything. What the sink does as it drains is the best clue.

One symptom does not prove a cause. If two rows seem to fit, start with the cheaper test.

Five tests to run before you call

Take them in this order, because each one rules something out without making a mess.

  1. 1

    Pull the stopper and look underneath

    In a bathroom sink, lift the stopper out or unscrew the pivot nut behind the drain tailpiece. A stopper that has slipped off its clevis can sit half closed and hold water back even when the pipe is clear.

  2. 2

    Fill the basin and let it go all at once

    A trickle test hides problems. A full basin draining at once pushes air and water through together and exposes a vent restriction as gurgling at the trap or at a nearby fixture.

  3. 3

    Open the trap over a bucket

    Loosen the slip nuts on the P-trap and look inside. Black sludge or a thin hair net across the trap arm explains it, while a clean trap shifts suspicion to the wall pipe or the vent.

  4. 4

    Run a second fixture

    Flush the toilet or run the tub. If the sink gurgles or its water level shifts, the fixtures share a branch line, and the blockage is past all of them.

  5. 5

    Check the disposal and dishwasher hose

    On a kitchen sink, check that the dishwasher hose forms a high loop under the counter. A new disposal with the dishwasher knockout plug left in place can also block that side.

If your tests point past the trap, call and tell us which of the symptoms in the table below matches what you see.

The vent is invisible until it fails

Vents run inside walls and out the roof, and they can clog with leaves, nests or debris that falls into the open pipe. You usually cannot confirm that from the ground, and climbing a roof to look is a task for a professional with fall protection.

A technician can confirm a vent problem by sending a camera or a cable through the roof opening, or by pressure testing, and that is one case where guesswork costs more than a call.

What you can do yourself and what to leave alone

You can safely adjust a pop-up stopper, clean a P-trap, flush the line with hot water, and clear the strainer basket of a kitchen sink. These cost nothing but time and do not affect any other part of the system.

Leave vent work, any cutting of pipe inside a wall and anything involving a main line to a licensed plumber. Gurgling in several fixtures at once, sewage odor and a toilet that bubbles are signs that the problem sits in the drain system itself. A professional drain cleaning with a camera can find what a household snake cannot, and a worn valve or seal under the sink may call for fixture and valve repair instead.

Avon note: how local water chemistry feeds slow drains

Citizens Water reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, and it varies by treatment plant, so ask Citizens for the figure at your address. Hard water combines with soap to form a scum that clings to trap walls, pop-up stoppers and strainer baskets.

That scum narrows the passage a little at a time, which is why a sink in a hard-water area can slow down with no single clump to pull out. If you notice white crust on faucets and aerators, expect a thin film in the trap too, and clean the stopper and trap on a schedule rather than waiting for a full stop.

Source type: Citizens Energy Group published water quality reports

Why the same sink might behave differently in a neighboring town

Older housing near a historic downtown, like the streets around the courthouse square in Danville, usually means older drain materials, and rough interior walls hold more buildup. Newer construction, like much of the housing Avon added after 2000, leans toward smooth plastic pipe, so a partial restriction there is more often soap and hair. A plumber serving Plainfield runs the same tests in the same order.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

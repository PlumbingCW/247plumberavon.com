How to Waterproof Your Basement Yourself in Avon, IN, and Where DIY Stops

By the Avon 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

A homeowner can fix most basement dampness from the outside in: gutters, downspouts, grading and window wells first, then crack sealing, then an interior drain and sump if water still comes through. Painting a sealer on the wall is the step most people try first and the one that fails most often.

This guide for Avon, IN homes ranks the DIY fixes by how likely they are to work, and marks the point where a plumber or foundation specialist should take over.

The mistake that wastes a weekend

Hardware stores sell waterproofing paint, and it looks like the obvious fix for a damp wall. The paint works on condensation and faint seepage. It does not hold back water pressing through the wall from wet soil, because that pressure peels the coating off in sheets.

Before spending anything, work out where the water comes from. The source decides the fix, and the cheapest fixes sit outside the house.

Find the source with three simple tests

You need dry weather for the first test and a rainy day for the second.

  1. 1

    The foil test

    Tape a one-foot square of aluminum foil tight to the wall and leave it for a day. Moisture under the foil means water coming through the wall. Moisture on top means indoor humidity.

  2. 2

    The rain watch

    During a downpour, walk the outside. Look for downspouts dumping at the foundation, gutters overflowing, and soil sloping toward the house.

  3. 3

    The floor check

    Look at where the floor meets the wall. Water that rises along the cove joint points to hydrostatic pressure from below, which needs drainage, not paint.

Outside work that does the heavy lifting

These fixes cost the least and cut the water load on the wall.

  • Clean gutters and add downspout extensions that discharge well away from the foundation
  • Slope soil away from the wall, about 6 inches of drop over the first 10 feet when the site allows it
  • Cover window wells and make sure they drain
  • Seal gaps where utility lines enter the wall
  • Keep mulch and planting beds from holding water against the foundation

What Avon homeowners should know about runoff and discharge

Avon runs a stormwater utility, and its fact sheet notes the town has seen significant population growth. Much of Avon was built during rapid growth after 2000, so yards often sit inside neighborhood drainage meant to carry water for many lots. Before regrading or redirecting a downspout, make sure you are not pushing runoff toward a neighbor or into a swale that serves the street.

Avon's sewer service comes from the West Central Conservatory District. Do not tie a sump pump discharge into a sanitary sewer line, and ask the district what it requires. Call the Avon Planning and Building Department about permit rules, and call 811 before digging a discharge trench.

Sealing a crack the right way

A hairline crack that weeps can be handled with an epoxy or polyurethane injection kit. Chip loose material, clean the crack, mount the injection ports on it, seal between them with paste, and inject from the lowest port up until resin shows at the next one.

Hydraulic cement suits a wet crack or a gap where a pipe passes through the wall, because it sets even under flowing water. Skip both if the crack keeps widening, since that points to movement that sealing cannot stop.

Interior drains and sump pumps: the plumbing side

When the foundation itself is taking on water, an interior drain tile collects it. A channel is cut along the floor perimeter, perforated pipe is bedded in stone, and the pipe runs to a sump basin where a pump lifts the water out through a discharge line.

This is plumbing and concrete work, and a mistake means a wet floor again. Our sump pump repair team can inspect an existing basin, and a basement waterproofing visit covers the drain and discharge plan. Residents in Brownsburg and Danville face the same questions.

When it is a foundation problem, not a plumbing one

Horizontal cracks, a bowing wall or stair-step cracks in block signal structural movement. A plumber can install drainage, but the wall needs a foundation or structural specialist. Be honest about that line with the contractor you hire, and ask them to say in writing what their scope covers.

What drives the price of a drainage fix

Compare itemized written prices from at least two licensed plumbers, because scope varies a lot.

Linear feet of drain

A perimeter drain along one wall is a smaller job than one around the whole basement.

Floor cutting and restoration

Cutting concrete, bedding pipe and patching the slab take most of the labor.

Sump basin and pump

Pump size, basin depth and whether a battery backup is included all change the total.

Discharge route

A longer or buried discharge line needs more pipe and digging, and a call to 811 first.

Permit questions

Ask the Avon Planning and Building Department what applies.

Homeowners policies typically respond to sudden and accidental damage and typically exclude seepage, groundwater and the failed part itself, so ask your carrier what water backup coverage you hold.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

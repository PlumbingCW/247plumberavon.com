Sewer backup: what to do first

These steps limit damage until the main line is open.

  • Stop flushing and stop every drain. Water you add goes to the lowest opening in the house.
  • Keep people and pets out of the wet area. Sewage water carries bacteria, and it should not touch skin or food surfaces.
  • Move belongings off the floor if you can do it without stepping into the water.
  • Stay away from outlets, cords and appliances in a flooded basement. If water reaches electrical equipment, leave it alone and call.
  • Locate the cleanout if you can. It is a capped pipe, often near the foundation or in a basement or crawl space, and knowing where it is saves time.
  • Do not pour chemicals into the drain. A plumber will be working through that opening.

Where the main line runs and where it fails

Every drain in the house joins a single main house drain, which exits through the foundation and runs out to the sewer lateral. The lateral carries waste to the utility's main in the street or easement. A blockage anywhere on that path backs up the whole house.

Most main line blockages form at a few predictable spots. Roots enter at joints. A sag, called a belly, collects solids at its low point. A joint that has shifted catches paper. Grease and wipes, including those labeled flushable, build up where the flow slows.

Opening the main sewer line

The work follows a fixed order so nothing is guessed.

  1. 1

    Confirm it is the main

    The plumber checks which fixtures back up and which ones drain, then decides whether the blockage is in the house drain or in the lateral.

  2. 2

    Open the cleanout

    A four-inch main takes a heavier cable than a sink drain. If the cleanout cap will not budge, the plumber works through a removed toilet instead.

  3. 3

    Break through and clear

    The cable cuts or hooks the blockage. A hydro jetter follows when the pipe wall is coated with grease or fine roots.

  4. 4

    Look at the pipe

    A camera pass shows the wall, the joints and any low spots. It takes little time and tells you whether the clog was a one-time event.

  5. 5

    Test and explain

    Fixtures are run together to prove the flow. You hear what was in the line and what to watch for.

Several drains slow or backing up at once? That points to the main line, so call now.

Who maintains which part of the sewer

The Town of Avon's utilities page lists West Central Conservatory District for sewer. Service boundaries vary by address, and the line between the district's responsibility and the homeowner's lateral differs from property to property, so confirm with the town before you assume.

A practical test: if neighbors on your street are backing up as well, the blockage may be in the district's main. In that case, call the district. If only your house backs up, the blockage is almost always on your side, and we can clear it.

Main line cleaning for different homes

The same symptom looks a little different depending on the house.

01

Homes built after 2000

Much of Avon grew quickly after 2000, so many houses have plastic sewer pipe. Plastic is smooth, so grease and wipes are the usual cause, along with construction debris and the occasional root from a maturing tree. These lines respond well to cable and jetting.

02

Older neighborhoods

Older housing near a historic downtown often has cast iron or clay tile, with rough walls and more joints. Roots and scale are common, and aggressive cleaning needs more care. Our Danville plumbing page explains what older neighborhoods usually involve.

03

Rentals and multi-unit buildings

Heavy daily use and mixed habits, including wipes and paper products, make backups more likely. A building with several units on one lateral can show backups on the lowest floor first.

04

Finished basements

A basement bathroom or bar sink is the first place a blocked main shows itself. Protect flooring, and call before the water reaches drywall or wiring.

Why a sewer line blocks again

A line that blocks twice in a year has a cause that cleaning does not remove. Roots that entered a joint will regrow after a cutter pass. A belly will collect solids again. A cracked pipe will keep letting soil in.

A camera look after cleaning, covered under sewer inspection, shows which one you have. If the footage shows damage, sewer line repair explains the options. If the pipe is sound and the cause is grease or wipes, the fix is in the household habits.

What not to flush

The usual offenders are wipes, paper towels, cat litter, feminine products and cooking fat poured down a sink. Labels that say flushable describe how a wipe breaks down in a lab, not how it behaves in a sag with slow flow. Anything that is not toilet paper belongs in the trash.

Fat is the quiet one. It leaves the kitchen as a liquid and hardens in the cooler pipe under the yard, where it traps everything else.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Cured-in-Place Pipe Lining in Avon, IN: What Drives the Cost and When It Fits

By the Avon 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Cured-in-place pipe lining (CIPP) repairs a damaged sewer pipe from the inside. A resin-soaked felt tube is pulled or inverted into the old pipe, inflated, and hardened into a new pipe within the old one, with no trench along its length.

It works only when the old pipe is still continuous and round enough to hold the liner, which a sewer camera inspection confirms. The price depends on length, diameter, access points and prep, so this guide shows how to compare itemized written prices in Avon, IN.

Lining is a repair, not a magic sleeve

A liner is only as good as the host pipe around it. If the pipe has collapsed, lost a section or dropped into a deep belly, the liner has nothing to cure against and the job becomes a dig. That is why every honest lining job begins with a survey, not a sales call.

How the lining process works

Most residential jobs follow the same sequence, even when crews use different resin systems.

  1. 1

    Clean and survey

    The pipe is jetted or cabled to remove scale, roots and debris, then scoped end to end with a camera. Defects are logged by distance.

  2. 2

    Prepare the liner

    A felt tube is cut to length and saturated with epoxy or polyester resin. Mixing and saturation happen at a controlled ratio, often in a vehicle or on a tarp.

  3. 3

    Insert and inflate

    The liner is pulled through or inverted with air or water pressure so it presses against the host wall. Branch openings are noted before it goes in.

  4. 4

    Cure

    Resin hardens with heat, steam or light, and the time depends on the system and pipe size. Do not use the drain during cure.

  5. 5

    Reinstate and verify

    A cutter reopens connections, and a final camera pass confirms a smooth, continuous wall.

Types of lining job

Not all lining is the same product, and the type depends on where the damage sits. Homeowners in Plainfield and Avon see the same range of options.

01

Lining a whole lateral

The liner runs from a cleanout to the connection point, covering every joint on the way. It suits pipe with several defects or scale along the full length.

02

Patch liner for one defect

A shorter sleeve covers a single crack or open joint. It is cheaper on materials but leaves older pipe on either side to deteriorate.

03

Connections and tees

Where the pipe turns or meets a branch, a lateral-reinstatement step cuts the opening back. These transitions are where careful crews spend extra time.

When lining is the wrong answer

A pipe with a bellied low section holds standing water, and a liner will copy that shape instead of fixing it. A collapsed section cannot take a liner at all. Heavy offsets at joints also block the tube from passing through.

If your pipe fails those tests, ask for a replacement price instead through our sewer line repair team. A good plumber will say so rather than push a method, and you should get the camera footage to back the call, whether you live in Avon or over in Danville.

What drives the price of lining

There is no single number for a lining job, because several things change from one house to the next.

Length and diameter

More footage and larger pipe means more resin, felt and cure time.

Access points

A clear cleanout cuts the work. Pits dug for access add excavation and restoration.

Prep and cleaning

Heavy scale, roots or old repairs must be cleared before the liner goes in.

Number of connections

Each branch or tee needs to be reinstated by cutter after cure.

Permit and inspection

Some work needs a permit. Ask the Avon Planning and Building Department what applies.

Homeowners policies typically respond to sudden and accidental damage and typically exclude wear, root intrusion and gradual deterioration, so confirm with your carrier before you assume coverage.

How to compare lining prices

Put each written price next to the others and check these items.

  • Footage lined and the diameter of the liner
  • Resin type and cure method
  • Cleaning and root removal listed as a separate line
  • Number of branches reinstated
  • Final camera footage included in the job
  • Restoration of any pits and surfaces
  • Permit handling and who pulls it

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

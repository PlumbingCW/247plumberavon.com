Active leak or backup at your business: what to do first

Whoever is on site can limit damage in the first few minutes.

  • Find the main water shutoff, usually near where the line enters the building or in a utility room, and close it fully.
  • Stop using restrooms, sinks and floor drains if wastewater is rising. Block off the area so nobody walks through it.
  • Turn off power at the breaker to wet rooms and move equipment, inventory and records above floor level.
  • Photograph the water and the damage before cleanup, and note the time you noticed it for your insurance file.
  • Call (317) 740-1302 and give the address, the tenant or suite, and whether you can reach the shutoff.

Commercial drains, grease and floor drains

Restaurants, salons and laundries load their drains differently than homes do. Grease from kitchens cools in the line and coats the pipe wall until the opening narrows, and hair and lint in other trades do the same.

We clear blockages with drain machines for the branch lines and a hydro jetter for built-up grease and sludge in larger lines, and we use a camera to see what is left behind. A recurring backup is a sign that cleaning on a schedule is cheaper than repeated emergencies. Where a grease trap or interceptor is installed, it needs regular pumping by the right contractor, and we tell you if that is the cause.

Commercial leak detection: what to expect, honestly

Many commercial leak calls come from property managers who already suspect a leak and want it located. Detection works well for a pressurized supply leak: we isolate sections, use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging, and a leak correlator on long runs.

The limits are real. Large buildings have long, hidden runs, and tenant spaces, ceilings and shared walls complicate access. We cannot diagnose a leak over the phone, and some detection jobs end with a location and a recommended repair that someone else, such as a roofer, a mechanical contractor or the landlord, has to authorize.

If you are comparing providers, ask what is being tested, what the report includes, and who decides on repairs. A short call to our leak detection team explains how the work is split.

Backflow testing for businesses

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination, and many commercial properties need them tested on a schedule.

  1. 1

    Find the assembly

    Backflow devices sit on the water service, irrigation lines and some equipment. We locate each one at your property.

  2. 2

    Test and record

    A certified test checks that the check valves and relief valves hold. Results are recorded for the water utility.

  3. 3

    Repair if it fails

    Worn seals or springs are replaced and the device is retested.

  4. 4

    Submit results

    Your utility sets the rules for reporting and deadlines. Check with it, since a missed test can mean the water to an irrigation line is shut off.

Storm drains and parking lots

Avon has a stormwater utility, and heavy rain exposes clogged catch basins and lot drains on commercial sites. Water pooling in a parking area or backing toward a loading dock is a storm drain symptom.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

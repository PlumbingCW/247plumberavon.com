How Long Does It Take to Install a Water Heater in Avon, IN?

By the Avon 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Installation time is decided before the plumber arrives, by what surrounds the old heater. A like-for-like swap of the same fuel, size and location is the shortest job. A new vent path, a gas line change, a fuel switch or a permit inspection adds steps, and any of them can stretch the work across more than one visit.

We do not promise hours before seeing the setup. This guide explains what the timeline depends on so you can plan around it.

What changes the timeline

The labor is similar across jobs, but the number of separate steps is not. This table shows which situations add time and why.

SituationWhy it takes longerWhen you learn it
Like-for-like swapDraining, disconnecting, setting and refilling are the whole jobAt the first call
Fuel switch, such as gas to electricNew circuit or gas capping, plus permit workAt the first call
New or longer ventingVent route, sealing and inspection take separate stepsOn site

Corroded valves or fittingsOld parts must be replaced before new ones go onAfter the old tank comes out

Why a simple swap can still become two visits

Most surprises appear after the old tank is out. Corroded shutoff valves, a brittle gas flex connector or a rusted vent section cannot be reused safely, and a missing part may mean a trip to the supply house.

A second visit also happens when a permit inspection must be passed before the job is considered done, or when a drain pan, expansion tank or electrical circuit has to be added.

None of this is predictable from the street. A photo of the heater, its vent and the surrounding pipes, plus the data plate, lets a technician flag likely extras before the day of the job.

The phases of a typical install

The order is the same on almost every job, and each phase has its own variables.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain the old tank

    Water, fuel and power are turned off. A tank with heavy sediment drains slowly, and a stuck drain valve can hold the job up.

  2. 2

    Disconnect and remove the old unit

    Gas or electrical connections, water lines, the relief line and the vent come off. The old tank is heavy when it still holds water, so stairs and tight turns matter.

  3. 3

    Set the new heater and connect it

    The new tank is leveled on a drain pan, then water, venting, relief line and fuel or power are connected, and an expansion tank is added when required.

  4. 4

    Fill and purge the air

    The tank fills with the hot tap open, which pushes air out of the lines. Skipping this step causes sputtering and poor first results.

  5. 5

    Start up and test

    The unit is lit or powered, the venting is checked and the relief valve is verified. A leak test and a safety check come before the old tank leaves.

  6. 6

    Wait for the tank to heat

    A new tank must heat a full volume of cold water, and the time depends on the fuel, the burner or element rating and the tank size.

Heat-up time is part of the schedule

Even a perfect install does not give hot water the moment the pilot lights. A 50-gallon tank starts full of cold water, and gas, electric and heat pump units recover at different speeds.

Plan showers and laundry for after the heater has had time to catch up, and leave the dishwasher off until you see steady hot water at a tap.

To map the steps for your own heater, call and describe where it sits and how it is vented.

Avon note: permits and inspection scheduling

The Town of Avon Planning and Building Department is at 6570 East US Highway 36, phone 317-272-0948. It is open Monday to Friday from 8 to 4:30 and closed daily from 11:30 to 12:30. Permit applications are submitted online through the town portal, and contractors use a contractor portal to schedule inspections.

So if your job needs a permit, the inspection depends on the department's weekday hours, not only on the plumber's calendar. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the department what applies to a water heater replacement or a fuel change before you pick a date.

How to prepare so the job goes smoothly

A few minutes of preparation can remove delays on the day.

  • Clear about three feet around the heater and a path to it, including stairs and doorways.
  • Locate and test the main water shutoff, and make sure it closes fully.
  • Photograph the data plate, the vent and the surrounding pipes for your records.
  • Plan to have no hot water for a stretch, and arrange showers and laundry around it.
  • Secure pets and move valuables out of the work area.
  • If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first.

What you can do yourself and what to leave to a plumber

You can prepare the space, clear a path, and take photos. You can also flush an old tank for maintenance, although a heater that is leaking should be replaced, not drained and kept.

Everything else involves fuel, venting and pressurized water, so it belongs to a licensed plumber. Read about water heater installation for a planned swap or water heater replacement when the old tank has failed. Customers in Brownsburg and Danville face the same variables and the same sequence.

Emergency swaps versus planned ones

A tank that has failed and is flooding a utility room changes the order of operations. Water is shut off, the area is made safe, and installation follows once the space is dry.

A planned swap lets you pick a day, settle the permit question and have parts ready. If your heater is old, replacing it before it fails removes the flood risk and gives you control of the calendar.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

Burst pipe in progress: the first steps

These steps limit the damage in the minutes before a plumber arrives.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the water heater, in the basement or crawlspace, or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house and the highest one to drain what is left in the lines and relieve pressure.
  • Switch off an electric water heater at the breaker so the element does not run dry. If you are unsure which breaker, leave it and tell us when you call.
  • Do not step into standing water near outlets, power strips or appliances. If water is reaching the panel, stay out and call the electric utility.
  • Photograph the damage and the pipe before you move anything, because the pictures help any insurance claim.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the wet area and soak up water with towels or a wet vacuum.

What actually makes a pipe burst

Freezing is the best known cause, but ice rarely splits the pipe where the ice sits. Water trapped between an ice plug and a closed faucet has nowhere to go, so pressure climbs until the weakest spot gives, often a few feet away. That is why a pipe can look fine while frozen and fail as it thaws.

Other causes are quieter. Corrosion thins metal from the inside, high pressure and water hammer fatigue fittings, a nail or screw from earlier work nicks a line, and ground movement stresses buried pipe. A failure at a joint is more common than a failure along a straight run, especially in newer plastic systems.

If a pipe froze once, expect more problems. Walls on an exterior side, garage ceilings, crawlspaces and rooms over unheated spaces are where it tends to recur, and we check those runs while the wall is open.

Repair approach by pipe material

What we do depends on what the pipe is made of and how it failed.

01

Copper pipe repair

Copper splits along the seam when frozen or pinholes from inside when corroded. A clean split is cut out and replaced with a new section, joined by solder or press fittings. Several pinholes on one run suggest the pipe is thinning, and we say so before patching it again.

02

PEX repair

PEX tolerates a freeze better than rigid pipe but fittings and crimp rings can still fail. We cut out the damaged section and join new pipe with approved fittings, so a repair here is usually quick. PEX exposed to sunlight or sitting against hot lines needs a closer look.

03

CPVC and PVC repair

These plastics turn brittle with age and heat, and a crack can run well beyond the visible break. We cut back to sound pipe, solvent weld new sections and test the joints once they cure. A pipe that cracks easily when cut is a sign other sections may follow.

04

Old steel and cast iron

Galvanized supply lines rust from the inside and cast iron drains crack or corrode through. Repairs here often reveal that the pipe on either side is also weak. If so, repiping the run may be more honest than a patch.

05

Broken drain pipe

A cracked drain leaks wastewater, not clean supply water, and it can wash soil from under a slab or yard. We confirm the break with a camera, cut out the damaged section and replace it with matching pipe at the correct slope.

How a burst pipe repair goes

The sequence below is the usual path, adjusted to what we find.

  1. 1

    Isolate and survey

    We confirm the shutoff is holding, check the wet area and trace the line to find where it failed. A moisture meter helps show how far the water traveled.

  2. 2

    Open only what we need

    Access might be a small cut in drywall or ceiling. We aim to expose enough pipe to work safely and keep the opening small.

  3. 3

    Cut out and replace

    The failed section is removed back to sound pipe and replaced with approved fittings and pipe of the same type or a compatible one.

  4. 4

    Pressure test and restore flow

    We bring the system back to pressure, watch for drips at each joint and check the other fixtures on that line. Drywall and paint are a separate trade, and we tell you which of the repairs fall to a restoration contractor.

The valve will not close, or there is no water at all

An old main shutoff that will not turn is a real emergency, because nothing stands between the break and your floor. Do not force a stuck valve with a wrench extension, since the stem can snap. Call us, and mention any second shutoff at the meter.

We replace seized or leaking shutoff valves so the next break is controllable. A pipe that fails with no visible water may be buried, and leak detection finds it.

Where the break is decides who fixes it

Inside the house, the pipe is yours. Outside, Citizens Energy Group states that property owners own the service line from the main to their building, so a break on that pipe is also an owner repair. A break in the street main is the utility's, and we can help you tell which you have.

Call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Avon Planning and Building Department whether your repair needs one.

Much of Avon was built during rapid growth after 2000, so many homes have newer supply pipe, and failed joints are a likelier culprit than rusted pipe. If the burst came from a water heater connection, water heater repair covers that side, and our Plainfield page covers the area to the south.

What shapes the cost of a burst pipe repair

Two pipe breaks that look the same on the surface can be very different jobs.

Location

An exposed line in a basement is a short job. A pipe in a finished wall or beneath concrete takes more time.

Pipe material

Copper, PEX and plastic each need different fittings and methods.

How far the damage extends

If neighboring sections are weak, replacing more of the run costs less than returning for another repair.

After-hours timing

Work on nights and weekends can be priced differently, and we state the clear price before work starts.

Water damage

Drying and rebuilding are separate from the plumbing repair.

Homeowners policies typically respond to sudden and accidental damage like a burst pipe and typically exclude wear, gradual leaks and the failed part itself, so ask your carrier what is covered. Photos of the damage help.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

Gas supply sizing: the step buyers skip

A tankless gas heater fires a large burner all at once, so it demands far more gas for a short period than a tank burner does. A line that served a tank heater well may be too small for the new unit, especially if it also feeds a furnace, a range and a dryer.

The plumber adds up the load on the line, measures the length and size of the pipe, and checks the meter and regulator limits. If the line comes up short, the fix is a larger gas line from a point along the run. Any smell of gas means everyone leaves and calls 911 or CenterPoint Energy at 1-800-227-1376 first.

Venting a tankless heater

Exhaust routing and materials depend on the unit.

01

Condensing units

High-efficiency units extract so much heat that exhaust is cool and acidic. They use plastic vent pipe, produce condensate and need a drain with a neutralizer, so the install needs a nearby drain or condensate pump.

02

Non-condensing units

These run hotter and use stainless steel vent material rated for the temperature. They need an appropriate vent run and clearances from the manufacturer's instructions.

03

Swapping a tank for tankless

The old tank's flue is rarely reusable, since tankless vent materials and routes differ. Plan for a new vent run through a wall or the roof and a combustion air path, as covered on our water heater installation page.

Signs your tankless heater needs attention

Several symptoms point to service rather than replacement.

  • Water runs hot, then cold, then hot again during a shower.
  • The display shows an error code, and the unit shuts down.
  • Hot water takes longer to arrive or the flow slows at fixtures.
  • You hear rumbling, popping or kettle-like noise during a heating cycle.
  • A pilot or ignition fails, or the unit cycles on and off.
  • Water is leaking at the unit, connections or relief valve.

Tankless water heater repair

Error codes tell the plumber where to start, but they are not a diagnosis. A code can point to a flame sensor, a flow sensor, a blocked vent, a scaled heat exchanger or a failing board, so the tech checks each against the manual for your model.

Clogged inlet screens, mineral scale and low gas pressure cause many calls. Some faults need the manufacturer's service network, and we will say so plainly. A leaking unit should have its water supply shut off, with gas off only if you smell no gas.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Scale is the usual reason a tankless heater loses output.

  1. 1

    Isolate the unit

    The plumber shuts off gas and power, then closes the isolation valves on the hot and cold lines. Units without service valves get them installed first.

  2. 2

    Connect the pump

    Hoses link the service ports to a small pump and a bucket of descaling solution, often white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner.

  3. 3

    Circulate

    The solution runs through the heat exchanger for an extended period to dissolve scale, with the plumber checking the color of the return.

  4. 4

    Rinse and restore

    The system is flushed with clean water, the inlet screen is cleaned, valves reopen and the unit is test fired.

Call with the model on the label and the symptom, and a plumber will say what the next step is.

Hard water and tankless units

Citizens Water reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, and it varies by treatment plant, so ask Citizens for the figure at your address. Hard water leaves scale inside a narrow heat exchanger, which is why many manufacturers call for periodic descaling and some households add a water softener. If your tap water is hard, tell us when you book, since it changes how often to flush.

What affects tankless pricing

Tankless jobs vary more than tank swaps, and these factors shape the quote.

Gas line size

A larger line, if the load requires it, adds pipe and labor.

Venting

New runs through walls or roofs add materials and time.

Condensate

Condensing models need a drain or pump for acidic condensate.

Water quality

Hard water may justify a softener or more frequent flushes.

Unit capacity

Larger units serve more fixtures at once and cost more.

Installation and wear-related service are typically a homeowner expense, while sudden and accidental water damage may be covered, so ask your carrier.

What to do if a tankless unit stops working

These steps are about safety first and damage control, not repair.

  • Check the display for an error code and note it before you call.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or CenterPoint Energy before doing anything else.
  • Do not open the vent or the sealed combustion cover yourself.
  • Close the cold supply valve if water is leaking from the unit, and switch off its power.

Plumbing a Double Sink Vanity in Avon, IN: Drains, Vents and Supply Lines

By the Avon 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Two sinks on one vanity share a single drain line in most homes. Each sink gets its own trap, the two trap arms join at a sanitary tee, and one vent serves both, so the layout works only if the wall drain and vent are in the right place and sized for the load.

This guide for Avon, IN homes shows the common layouts, what a plumber checks before a swap, and which steps a handy homeowner can take on.

Three ways to plumb two sinks

The layout depends on where the wall drain sits and how much room the cabinet gives you.

01

One wall stub, a tee in the cabinet

The most common setup. Both traps feed a horizontal trap arm that meets a sanitary tee, then a single branch enters the wall at the existing drain.

02

One stub per sink

Each sink has its own wall drain. This avoids a long shared arm and drains faster, but it means opening the wall to add a stub and a vent connection.

03

One central stub

A drain centered between the sinks suits a shared tee. It limits the cabinet design to sinks spaced evenly on either side.

Why venting is the part that goes wrong

A drain needs air behind the water. Without a vent, the water leaving one sink can pull the trap seal from the other, which causes gurgling, slow draining and a sewer odor from the dry trap. The vent also lets the line drain without sucking back.

Indiana's plumbing code, 675 IAC 16, sets the rules for trap arm length and vent sizing. A trap arm that runs too far, or too steeply, before reaching a vent will drain badly even when everything is hand tight.

What a plumber checks before the install

These checks keep a vanity swap from becoming a wall repair.

  1. 1

    Measure the rough-in

    The drain stub height and the distance from the wall, plus the supply stubs for hot and cold, must line up with the new cabinet.

  2. 2

    Check the vent

    The vent location and size decide whether a shared drain will work as laid out.

  3. 3

    Trial fit the traps

    Traps and arms go in dry first, so the plumber can confirm slope, about a quarter inch per foot, before cementing joints.

  4. 4

    Connect supply lines

    Angle stops, braided supply lines and faucets get hand tightened, then snugged with a wrench.

  5. 5

    Fill and test

    Both sinks are filled and drained together, so the plumber can watch for gurgling and leaks.

Hard water and permits for an Avon vanity

Citizens Water reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, and it varies by treatment plant, so ask Citizens for the figure at your address. Hard water leaves scale in faucet aerators and cartridges, which is a good reason to choose faucets with removable aerators and to keep a small bottle of descaler at hand.

For permit questions on a bathroom remodel, ask the Avon Planning and Building Department, since permit rules vary by job. Indiana licenses plumbing contractors, and you can verify a license at mylicense.in.gov. Supply lines, shutoffs and WaterSense faucets

Each faucet needs its own pair of angle stops, so you can shut off one sink without losing the other. Faucets with the EPA WaterSense label use less water, which helps when two sinks run at the same time. Replace rubber supply hoses with braided lines when the vanity comes out.

What a homeowner can do and when to call

Swapping a faucet and the visible traps on a vanity where the wall stubs line up is a reasonable DIY job. Take photos first, measure twice, and have the water shut off at the angle stops.

Call a plumber if the drain or vent must move, if you find corroded pipe in the wall, or if two sinks drain slowly after the swap.

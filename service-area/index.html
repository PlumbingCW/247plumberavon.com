Plainfield, about 5 miles south

Plainfield, ZIP 46168, is a larger town of 34,625 people in the 2020 census. US 40 is Main Street, and I-70 runs along its southern edge. Plainfield Water Works serves customers from groundwater with chlorine as the disinfectant, so its water differs from Avon's.

That matters for scale questions, since a hardness answer in Avon does not carry over. Ask Plainfield Water Works for its hardness figure at 317-839-3490 before choosing a water softener.

Brownsburg, about 6 miles north

Brownsburg, ZIP 46112, had 28,973 residents in 2020. White Lick Creek runs through town just west of the center, which is useful to know when a basement stays damp after rain. A wet basement near a creek corridor may be a drainage or sump pump issue before it is a pipe issue.

Brownsburg Water Works runs the municipal utility, and no hardness numbers are confirmed here, so ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

Danville, about 7 miles west on US 36

Danville, ZIP 46122, is the county seat of Hendricks County and had 10,559 residents in 2020. Older neighborhoods near a historic downtown usually mean older drain and supply materials than newer subdivisions around Avon. That raises the odds that a camera inspection or repiping question is a real one.

State Road 39 meets US 36 downtown. For hardness or quality details, ask your water utility, since we do not list a figure for Danville.

How the distance estimates work

The mileage in the table is the straight-line distance from Avon to the center of each town. Roads, construction and traffic stretch real trips, which is why we say about and roughly and never quote drive times.

No arrival time is promised on any call, in any town. The plumber tells you what to shut off while you wait, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

What changes by town, in practical terms

Same trade, different local details.

  • Water utility: Avon uses Citizens Water, Plainfield uses Plainfield Water Works, Brownsburg runs its own water works. Ask the right one for hardness and quality reports.
  • Sewer: Avon's sewer is the West Central Conservatory District, so a main backup there may involve district-owned pipe, and your service line is yours.
  • Creeks and drainage: White Lick Creek touches Brownsburg and Plainfield, which makes sump pumps and drain tile common conversation topics.
  • Housing age: much of Avon was built during rapid growth after 2000, while older areas near historic downtowns may carry older pipe.
  • Permits: rules vary by job and by town, so start with the building department where the work happens.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

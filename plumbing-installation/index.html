Planned plumbing, and where it differs from repair

Repair fixes what broke. Installation decides where pipe goes, how big it is, what slope drains need, and how fixtures connect, and those decisions are hard to change once walls and floors are closed.

That is why the best time to call a plumber on a remodel is before framing is finished or drywall goes up. Moving a drain by two feet at the rough-in stage is a short job. Moving it afterward means opening finished surfaces.

What we install

These are the usual jobs. Each has its own checks.

01

New and replacement fixtures

Sinks, toilets, tubs, showers, faucets and laundry boxes. Replacing a fixture in the same place is usually quick. Changing type, such as swapping a tub for a walk-in shower, often changes the drain and the valve.

We confirm rough-in dimensions and drain size before the fixture is bought, so it fits.

02

Remodel rough-in

Kitchens, baths, basements and laundry rooms. The plumber sets drains, vents and supply stubs where the fixtures will sit, then returns to set the fixtures after the finish work.

Venting is the part owners overlook. A drain without a proper vent gurgles and can siphon its trap dry, so the vent path is planned on day one.

03

New construction

On a new house, plumbing happens in stages: underground drains and sleeves before the slab or footing, rough-in when framing is up, then trim-out when finishes are done. Inspections are tied to each stage.

Ask the Avon Planning and Building Department about permits and inspection scheduling, since the requirements depend on the job.

04

Basement bathrooms

A basement bath below the sewer level needs thought, because drains cannot run downhill into a main that sits higher. Options include a sewage ejector pit and pump.

We will tell you whether your layout works with gravity or needs a pump before you commit to a design.

Low water pressure: finding where it is lost

Low pressure at every fixture points to the house's incoming side. Low pressure at one fixture points to that fixture, usually a clogged aerator, cartridge or partially closed valve.

We put an inline pressure gauge on a hose bib to read static pressure, then check the pressure regulator, if the house has one, and the main shutoff valve, which is sometimes only partly open after past work. A failing regulator, a corroded section of pipe, or a leak in the service line can all produce the symptom.

Mineral scale matters here too. Citizens Water reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, and it varies by treatment plant, so ask Citizens for the figure at your address. Scale narrows old pipe and clogs aerators, which is worth ruling out. Corroded supply lines may lead to our repiping page.

Gas smell or suspected gas leak: leave first

If you smell gas, or hear a hiss near a gas line or appliance, leave the building immediately and call 911 or the gas utility first. CenterPoint Energy is the gas utility listed on the Town of Avon's local utilities page, and its number there is 1-800-227-1376.

Do not turn lights or switches on or off, and do not use a phone inside. We do not promise a response time for gas leaks, because the utility and emergency services come first. Once the utility has made the area safe, we can help with plumbing-side gas piping work.

Gas line installation and inspection

Gas piping for a new range, dryer, fireplace or water heater is planned work, and it follows the code and the utility's rules. Pipe size depends on the total load of the appliances and the length of the run, so we calculate it for your layout.

Every gas connection is pressure tested before it is put into service, and fittings are checked for leaks with approved methods, not by smell. Our water heater installation page covers gas and electric venting details.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

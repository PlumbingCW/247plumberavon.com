A disposal that hums and will not turn

The hum without movement is the most common disposal call. Something hard, such as a bone, a fruit pit or a bottle cap, has wedged between the spinning plate and the shredder ring, and the motor is stalled against it.

A reset button on the bottom of the unit trips when this happens. Pressing it before the jam is cleared only cooks the motor, so the sequence matters: power off, clear the jam, wait, then reset.

Most jams free with a hex key turned in the socket at the bottom of the disposal. If the plate will not rotate by hand with the key, or the motor is silent when switched on, the problem is electrical or a seized motor, and a different repair applies.

Jammed or leaking right now: what to do first, and never use your hand

Disposal injuries come from trying to fish out a jam by hand, even with the power off at the switch. Use these steps instead.

  • Turn the wall switch off, then unplug the disposal from the outlet under the sink or switch off its breaker if it is hard-wired.
  • Use tongs or pliers to lift anything visible, never fingers.
  • Insert the hex key into the socket at the base of the unit and rock it back and forth until the plate turns freely.
  • Wait a few minutes, press the red reset button, then restore power and run cold water.
  • If it hums again, switch it off and leave it for a plumber. Repeated resets damage the motor.

Why a disposal leaks, and where

Where the water appears under the sink tells the plumber which seal failed.

01

Leak at the sink flange

The flange is the ring set in the sink drain opening, held by a mounting assembly and plumber's putty or a gasket. When water drips from the top of the disposal where it meets the sink, the seal has failed or the mounting ring has loosened.

The disposal is lifted off, the flange is re-sealed, and the assembly is tightened evenly. If the sink surface is cracked there, the fix is different.

02

Leak from the housing

Water running down the side or dripping from the underside of the unit points to a failed internal seal or a cracked housing. Those cannot be repaired.

A disposal leaking from the body is replaced, and the sooner it goes the less water gets into the cabinet floor.

03

Leak at the drain connection

The discharge elbow bolts to the side of the disposal with a gasket, and the trap beneath carries the waste on. A loose or worn gasket drips at these joints.

This is usually a gasket replacement or a refit of the pipe, not the whole unit.

04

Leak at the dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain hose attaches to a small inlet on the disposal. A corroded clamp or a cracked hose drips onto the cabinet floor only while the dishwasher is draining.

Run the dishwasher with a towel underneath to see when it appears, and tell us what you find.

Dishwasher connection and the air gap

A dishwasher drains into the disposal or into the sink trap, and the way it is connected prevents dirty water from flowing back. Some homes use an air gap, a small cylinder on the sink or counter. Others use a high loop in the hose, which raises the hose under the counter so wastewater cannot siphon back.

If water bubbles out of the air gap or the dishwasher fills with gray water, the line between the air gap and the disposal is usually clogged with food. We clear it, and when we replace a disposal we knock out the dishwasher plug inside the new unit so the dishwasher can drain.

Skipping that knockout is a common DIY mistake. The dishwasher then holds water because its drain has nowhere to go.

How a disposal replacement goes

The sequence, so there are no surprises.

  1. 1

    Power off and disconnect

    We kill the circuit, unplug or disconnect the wires, and loosen the discharge pipe and dishwasher hose.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit

    The mounting ring is released with a twist and the disposal comes down. The old flange and putty are cleaned away.

  3. 3

    Mount the new one

    A new flange is seated in sealant or gasket, the mounting assembly is tightened evenly, and the disposal hangs from it.

  4. 4

    Reconnect and test

    The drain pipe, dishwasher hose and power go back on. We run water, check every joint for drips, and test the dishwasher drain.

What goes down the sink, and what should not

Disposals handle soft scraps well and fail on fibrous stringy items like celery and corn husks, and on grease. Grease is the worst because it coats the pipe downstream, then cools and hardens into a blockage that no disposal can grind.

A clog in the line after the disposal needs drain cleaning, not a new motor. If the whole line backs up, the problem is usually further along, and the sewer line should be examined.

Hard water shortens a disposal's life

Citizens Water reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, and it varies by treatment plant, so ask Citizens for the figure at your address. Mineral scale builds on the grinding ring and in the discharge elbow, and it narrows the pipe where food scraps then catch.

Running cold water for a few seconds after use clears scraps. A softener, covered at water softener installation, reduces scale on everything the water touches.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

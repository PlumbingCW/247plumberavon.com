Emergency plumber dispatch: what to do before we arrive

The first few minutes decide how much damage a leak does. These four steps cost nothing and work while you are on the phone with us.

Stop the water

Close the shutoff valve closest to the leak, usually under a sink, behind a toilet or at the water heater. If nothing local closes it, shut the main valve where the line enters the house near the meter. Then open one low faucet to relieve pressure.

Gas smell means leave

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or CenterPoint Energy from outside. Do not flip switches or search for the source. We do not diagnose gas leaks by phone.

Keep electricity away from water

Water near outlets, a panel, a sump pump plug or an electric water heater is a shock risk. Do not wade through standing water to reach a breaker. If the panel is in a wet area, wait for help.

Protect and document

Lift rugs, move electronics and pull furniture off wet floors. Photograph the damage and note where the water first showed up, since your insurer will ask.

What our plumbers fix, by problem family

Each tab covers one family of jobs and points to the page with the full detail.

01

No hot water, a leaking tank or a planned upgrade

A tank that drips from the bottom is usually finished, and replacing a failed water heater is the fix. A tank that leaks from a fitting, or one that simply will not heat, is often a water heater repair involving an element, thermostat or valve.

Planned swaps go through water heater installation, including venting and expansion tank decisions. If you want endless hot water, the tankless water heater page covers sizing and descaling.

02

Slow drains, backups and mainline trouble

A single clogged fixture is a job for drain cleaning with a cable machine. When grease, scale or roots keep bringing a line back, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the blockage.

Sewage in a basement drain or several fixtures gurgling at once points to the main. Sewer line cleaning clears it, a camera sewer inspection shows why it clogged, and sewer line repair fixes a broken or bellied pipe.

03

Water where it should not be

A spraying or split pipe needs burst pipe repair right away. A hidden leak, such as an unexplained high bill or a stain growing on a ceiling, needs leak detection with acoustic and moisture tools before anyone cuts drywall.

Basements add two more causes. A failed pump calls for sump pump repair, and a basement that stays damp even when plumbing is sound may involve basement waterproofing and drainage work.

04

Toilets, faucets and disposals

A running or leaking toilet usually traces to a fill valve, flapper or wax ring, which is why toilet repair often ends in one visit. A faucet that drips or a shutoff valve that will not close falls under faucet repair.

A kitchen disposal that hums, jams or leaks underneath is a garbage disposal repair or replacement decision, based on the age of the unit and where the leak is.

05

Planned work and business properties

New fixtures, added lines and low water pressure traces belong to plumbing installation. Older pipe that keeps failing in different spots may justify repiping, though newer Avon homes rarely need it.

Hard water problems lead to water softener installation. Shops, offices and rental buildings use commercial plumbing service, including after-hours calls.

Plumbing in Avon: what the town's growth and water mean for you

Avon grew from 6,248 residents in 2000 to 21,474 in 2020, so much of the housing was built during that rapid growth. How to check any plumber's license with the Indiana PLA

Plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers in Indiana are licensed by the Indiana Plumbing Commission, administered by the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency. You can look up a name or license through the PLA's verify-a-license tool at mylicense.in.gov. Do this for any plumber before they start, including us.

Helpers and apprentices working under a licensed plumber do not need their own license, so ask who is the licensed plumber on your job. Indiana plumbing work also follows the state plumbing code, 675 IAC 16.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

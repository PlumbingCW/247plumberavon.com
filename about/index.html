How a job is scoped from first call to final check

Every job follows the same order, whether it is a dripping valve or a flooded basement.

  1. 1

    Describe the problem by phone

    You tell us what you see, where the water is and what you have already shut off. We ask about fixtures involved, recent changes and the age of the equipment.

  2. 2

    Diagnosis on site

    The plumber finds the cause before proposing a fix. That may mean a camera on a drain, a pressure gauge on a supply line or a moisture meter on a wall.

  3. 3

    Options and a clear price

    You hear the repair choices, the trade-offs between them and the price before work starts. If the cheapest fix is a patch that may fail, the plumber says so.

  4. 4

    Repair and verification

    After you approve the plan, the plumber does the work and tests it under real pressure or flow. You get an explanation of what changed and what to watch.

Diagnosis first, because guessing costs you twice

A dripping ceiling is not always a leaking pipe above it. Condensation, a failed shower pan or a roof leak can produce the same stain. Replacing a pipe without confirming the source means paying for the repair and then for the real fix.

That is why leak detection uses listening equipment and moisture readings before anyone opens a wall. The same logic applies to a backed-up line, where a camera inspection shows whether the problem is grease, roots or a collapsed section before anyone recommends digging.

When a clog is simple, drain cleaning with a cable machine may be all that is needed. When it is not, you should hear that before the cable goes in.

How to verify any plumber's license in Indiana

Plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers in Indiana are licensed by the Indiana Plumbing Commission under IC 25-28.5, and the Professional Licensing Agency administers the process. Homeowners can verify a license through the PLA's verify-a-license tool at mylicense.in.gov.

Helpers and apprentices who do not present themselves as plumbers do not need their own license, so it is fair to ask who the licensed plumber on your job is. Use this check on every contractor you hire, including us.

Standards we cite, and how we cite them

Indiana plumbing work follows the state plumbing code at 675 IAC 16, and model plumbing codes inform it. EPA WaterSense labels some faucets, toilets and showerheads that use less water. These are industry standards, not business certifications, and we mention them to give you something checkable.

Before any digging near a yard line, call 811. For permits on larger jobs such as a new water heater installation or a sewer replacement, permit rules vary by job, so ask the Avon Planning and Building Department at 317-272-0948.

Where we work and how that shapes the job

Our base is Avon, ZIP 46123, and we also serve Plainfield, Brownsburg and Danville. Each town has its own water utility, so a hardness or lead question in Plainfield has a different answer than one in Avon.

Avon's water comes from Citizens Water, and Citizens reports system-wide hardness averaging about 19 grains per gallon, varying by plant. Because that figure is not specific to Avon, ask Citizens for your address before deciding on a softener. Our service area page covers the rest of what changes by town.

Why the business is call-only

A plumbing problem is hard to describe in a box on a screen. A call lets the plumber ask which valve you can reach, whether the water is clear or brown, and how long it has been running. Those answers change the first steps and the tools brought to the job.

Calling also means you hear a real answer to a real question, such as whether tonight's problem can wait for a daytime visit. Some can, and we will say so. The contact page lists what to have ready.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

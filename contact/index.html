In an emergency, do these before you dial

A burst pipe or backed-up line does damage by the minute. These steps take under a minute each.

Close the nearest valve

Find the shutoff under the sink, behind the toilet or at the water heater. If none works, shut the main valve near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Leave if you smell gas

Get everyone out, then call 911 or CenterPoint Energy from outside. Do not use switches, lighters or a phone inside. We do not diagnose gas leaks by phone.

Stay out of standing water near power

Water around a panel, outlet, sump pump plug or electric water heater can shock you. Wait for help rather than wading in.

Stop using the drains

If sewage is rising or several fixtures gurgle, stop flushing and running water. Every flush adds to the overflow.

What to have ready when you call

You do not need all of these. Whatever you can gather helps the plumber decide on tools and first steps.

  • The service address, including the nearest cross street or landmark such as US 36 or Avon Avenue.
  • What you see, hear or smell, and when it started.
  • Which valves or breakers you have already turned off.
  • For a water heater: gas or electric, tank or tankless, and roughly how old it is.
  • For a drain problem: which fixtures are affected and whether the basement floor drain is involved.
  • Whether you rent, own or manage the property, since a landlord may need to approve work.
  • A callback number that works if the call drops.

Which problem is yours? A quick routing guide

A cold shower or a puddle at the tank belongs to water heater repair. Sewage or slow draining in several places points toward sewer line cleaning. A single slow sink is usually drain cleaning.

A split pipe or a wet wall is covered by burst pipe repair. You do not need to pick the right category before calling, since the plumber sorts it out. The full services list is there if you want to read ahead.

Verify a plumber before you let anyone in

Indiana licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers through the Professional Licensing Agency. Use the verify-a-license tool at mylicense.in.gov, and ask who the licensed plumber on your job is. This works for any contractor, including us.

Permits, utilities and who to call besides us

Some problems belong with someone else first. A gas smell goes to 911 and CenterPoint Energy. A water main break in the street goes to Citizens Water. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Avon Planning and Building Department at 317-272-0948, open weekdays 8 to 4:30 and closed daily 11:30 to 12:30.

If you plan to dig for a buried line, call 811 first. Underground utilities are not always where you expect.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.

How a hydro jetter clears a line

A jetting machine pumps water through a flexible hose to a nozzle with several small jets. Most of those jets point backward, so the water pulls the hose forward through the pipe while it blasts the walls. One jet points ahead to break up the blockage in front.

Pressure is measured in psi, and more is not always better. A plumber matches the nozzle and pressure to the pipe size and material. The water that does the cleaning then carries the debris down the line toward the main.

Jetting versus cabling

Neither tool wins everywhere. The pipe and the blockage choose.

When a backup is urgent: signs that point to jetting

One or two of these suggest the pipe is narrowed, not just blocked. If water is already coming up, stop using drains and call.

  • A drain cleared by cable slowed down again within weeks.
  • Gurgling in a toilet or tub when another fixture drains.
  • A floor drain that smells sour or burps after laundry.
  • Several fixtures slow together, with no single clog to blame.
  • A kitchen waste line or rental unit with heavy daily use.
  • A known history of grease or root trouble on the lateral.

What the jetter takes out of a pipe

Each kind of buildup behaves differently under water pressure.

01

Grease and soap

Fat and soap harden in layers, narrowing the bore. Jetting with hot water softens and shears those layers off the wall, which a cable cannot do. Kitchen lines and laundry branches are the common targets.

02

Fine and thick roots

Hair-thin roots enter through joints and look for water. Jetting flushes them out, but thick roots need a cutter head first, then the jetter to clean up. Roots usually return, so a camera look after cleaning shows where they enter.

03

Mineral scale and sludge

Old iron lines collect rough scale that snags paper and waste. Jetting knocks the loose layer off. It will not restore a badly corroded pipe, and a camera shows whether the iron underneath is still sound.

04

Sand, silt and debris

A belly, which is a low spot in the pipe, collects sediment that cable skips over. Jetting drives it out of the pipe. If the same spot refills, the belly itself is the problem and needs sewer line repair.

When we do not jet a line

Water pressure on a cracked, offset or collapsed pipe can wash soil out around the break and make it larger. If a cable stops at a hard point every time, or the line has a history of leaks, we look with a camera first through a sewer inspection and decide after that.

Honest limits: jetting cleans, it does not repair. A pipe with a hole in it stays holed, and the cleaning only shows how bad the hole is.

A jetting visit from start to finish

Each stage has a reason.

  1. 1

    Find a cleanout

    The hose goes in through a cleanout or an opened fixture. A cleanout is a capped pipe near the foundation or in a basement or crawl space.

  2. 2

    Open the blockage

    If the line is fully blocked, a cable or a forward-jetting nozzle goes first. Water cannot clean what it cannot reach.

  3. 3

    Scour in passes

    The nozzle moves forward and is pulled back slowly, washing the wall on the way out. Two or three passes are normal on a coated line.

  4. 4

    Camera the result

    A short camera run shows whether the wall is clean and whether roots, a crack or a sag explain the original trouble.

  5. 5

    Test the flow

    Water runs at several fixtures together to confirm the line carries a full load, not just a trickle.

Before the jetter arrives

A little preparation keeps the work clean and fast.

Clear the cleanout

Move boxes and shelving from the cleanout. If you cannot find it, say so on the call and the plumber will look for it.

Stop all water use

No flushing, no laundry, no showers. Water poured into a blocked line goes to the lowest fixture.

Protect the floor

Lay old towels or plastic near the access point. Jetting can throw some water back at the opening.

Note the history

Tell us about earlier cleanings and any pipe repairs. That history decides pressure and the order of the work.

What drives the cost of a jetting job

The price depends on the line, not the machine.

Line length and size

A short four-inch lateral is a smaller job than a long run with several bends.

Severity of buildup

A lightly coated pipe may need one pass. Heavy grease or roots take repeated passes and a cutter head.

Access

A cleanout makes the job direct. Without one, the plumber may need to pull a toilet to get the hose in.

Camera work

A recording pass before or after adds time but removes guesswork about what comes next.

After-hours timing

An overnight or holiday backup is priced differently from a planned daytime visit, and you hear the number before work starts.

Jetting on Avon lines

Much of Avon was built during rapid growth after 2000, and homes from that period generally have plastic drain and sewer pipe. Plastic tolerates jetting well, and the usual targets are kitchen grease and young tree roots working into joints on mature lots. Older houses, like those near a historic downtown, tend to have iron or clay pipe that needs more care.

The Town of Avon's utilities page lists West Central Conservatory District for sewer, but service boundaries vary by address, so confirm with the town where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins. We handle the line on your side of that point. If neighbors are backing up too, the blockage may be in the district's main, and that is a call to the district.

Codes, licensing and 811

Plumbing work in Indiana follows the state plumbing code (675 IAC 16), and the Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors and journeyman plumbers. You can check any plumber through the license lookup at mylicense.in.gov before work starts, and you should call 811 before any digging.

Codes and standards decide what is allowed on a given job, so ask which ones apply to yours.
